# Active listening for sales discovery

Use this reference for call coaching, transcript critique, or role-play feedback. It is not a separate broadly triggered skill because listening is a method inside discovery, objection handling, and negotiation rather than an independent commercial outcome.

## Observable techniques

- **Pause:** allow the speaker to finish; do not use silence as pressure.
- **Reflect:** restate the substance in fewer words and ask whether it is accurate.
- **Clarify:** ask for an example, definition, frequency, or consequence.
- **Separate:** distinguish what was said from what the seller thinks it means.
- **Test:** offer a tentative interpretation and an easy correction: “I may be reading this wrong—does X matter because Y, or is something else driving it?”
- **Summarize:** group only points the speaker confirmed; include unresolved contradictions.

## Evidence-aware note taking

Record compactly:

| Item | What to capture |
| --- | --- |
| Direct statement | Short quote or faithful paraphrase plus timestamp, message, or note reference |
| Observable fact | What was directly shown and where |
| Interpretation | Seller hypothesis and why it might be wrong |
| Open question | Neutral question that would resolve the uncertainty |

Do not label tone, hesitation, pace, or omitted topics as facts. Cultural style, disability, neurodiversity, language fluency, connection quality, and ordinary reflection can all affect conversational signals.

## Coaching a recording or transcript

Check only observable behavior:

1. Did the seller interrupt or answer their own question?
2. Did follow-ups respond to the buyer's last answer, or merely follow a script?
3. Were summaries verified by the buyer?
4. Did the seller explore evidence that challenged the assumed fit?
5. Were decisions, risks, and next steps attributed accurately?

Confirm the user has an appropriate basis to use the recording or transcript. Do not reproduce unnecessary personal details, and do not provide jurisdiction-specific recording-law conclusions without current authoritative research or qualified advice.
